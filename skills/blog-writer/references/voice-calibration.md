# Voice Calibration

The author-preservation contract applies to every writing or rewriting assignment.
Personal voice calibration applies when a personal identity is selected. Corporate-only
writing reports voice calibration as `not applicable`; it still receives preservation,
generic tone, and corporate editorial checks.

## Establish the author-preservation contract

Before the first draft or rewrite, keep the source and record a short contract in the
research memory bank, or in working notes for a scoped edit:

- The claim the author wants the reader to consider and the author's stance toward it.
- How the argument progresses, including the job of each paragraph in a short artifact.
- Author-supplied or explicitly endorsed phrases whose attitude, humor, or rhetorical
  function matters. Quote their wording and identify their source in this conversation
  or supplied material. Record a function, not a quota of jokes or devices.
- Requested format, grammatical person, audience, and scope of change.

Use the author's current framing and corrections as assignment evidence even when formal
voice calibration remains unresolved. Distinguish author-written text, endorsed wording,
and unapproved assistant drafts. Approval of an assistant draft establishes an assignment
preference; it does not establish independent evidence of personal authorship. Do not invent
an attitude or a distinctive phrase when the source supplies neither. A neutral assignment
can remain neutral. Update the contract when the author changes direction.

Third person changes pronouns, not the author's evidenced stance or register. A named
technical reference can carry an argument; do not replace it with a generic lesson merely
to make the prose smoother. For a short abstract, check that the format explanation forms
a coherent unit and that examples develop the argument without interrupting that unit.
Apply paragraph functions from the assignment; do not prescribe one universal structure.

## Verify preservation after rewriting

Compare source and revised passages against the contract after each completed editing pass, including
changes made for a mechanical finding or corporate review. Compare against the original
assignment sources as well as the pre-edit draft; an earlier rewrite may already have
lost intent. Repeat for consequential changes, not every keystroke. Record which passage carries
each protected function and why any protected wording changed. Grammar and factual
corrections may change the wording; retain the stance and argumentative function wherever
the evidence and assignment permit. An unsupported factual claim still requires correction.

A preservation check fails when a source-backed opinion becomes a neutral topic summary,
a joke loses its target or self-implication, an example interrupts the promised explanation,
or concrete actions become corporate abstractions. Repair the affected passage before calling the result ready. A clean scanner result
cannot override this failure. Apply `skills/blog-writer/references/sweep-review.md` to
scanner findings; a contextual count need not be eliminated to complete the assignment.
Do not repair sentence counts by adding filler or discarding a meaningful line. Keep unaffected wording when the requested edit is local.

Record `Author preservation: pass` only with the source-to-revision comparison above.
Record `needs repair` for a known lost function and `unresolved` when essential intent
is missing. Name the affected passage or gap. Ask only when that missing intent changes
the requested edit; do not interrupt routine edits to obtain formal calibration samples. Missing same-mode
calibration passages alone does not prevent preservation of wording and intent already
supplied for this assignment. Keep formal voice calibration unresolved separately.

## Establish usable evidence

A usable calibration passage is contiguous, multi-sentence prose that shows how the author
moves between thoughts. It must expose cadence, paragraph transitions, narrator presence,
reader relationship, or argument movement. A local excerpt resource is usable directly. A
canonical URL is usable only after its prose has been fetched and read.

These are not usable passages on their own:

- a URL with no fetched prose
- a one-line device summary
- an isolated punchline or quotation
- a list of rhetorical devices
- a source whose authorship or editorial history is unknown

Substantial author-written passages supplied in the current conversation can qualify.
Apply the same mode and authorship requirements; do not count an assistant draft as
author-written evidence merely because the author approved it.

Name the assignment mode before selecting evidence. Use a concrete mode such as failure
story, analytical opinion, tutorial, announcement, or product explanation. Select two or
three passages in the same mode. Use the nearest mode only when the research bank records
the mismatch.

For each passage record:

- source title and local path, URL, or other stable locator
- passage location inside the source
- writing mode
- authorship as `solely authored`, `collaborative`, or `heavily edited`
- the voice dimensions the passage demonstrates

Set calibration readiness to `ready` only when two or three usable passages match the
assignment mode. Set it to `unresolved` when the evidence is missing, inaccessible, has
unknown authorship, or covers only a different mode. Name the missing evidence. Ask for suitable passages when personal voice matching is
part of the requested work; continue a routine edit using available assignment evidence
without a new approval gate. Keep `unresolved`; never infer readiness from identity approval.

Record the mode, readiness, and passage inventory under `## Voice Calibration` in the
research memory bank. Do not copy whole published works into the bank. A locator plus the
specific passage boundaries and analysis is enough.

## Compare the draft

Keep the pre-edit draft available for comparison. After each anti-pattern editing pass,
compare the pre- and post-rewrite prose with the selected passages on five dimensions:

- narrator presence: where and how the author enters the argument
- spoken cadence: sentence grouping, pauses, acceleration, and emphasis
- connective paragraph flow: how one paragraph carries thought into the next
- reader relationship: directness, assumed knowledge, and distance
- argument movement: how claims are introduced, qualified, supported, and revisited

Rhetorical devices are evidence, never quotas. Naming three devices does not establish
calibration. Adding a joke, aside, or punchline to generic analyst prose does not repair the
five dimensions above.

Report `Voice calibration: calibrated` only when readiness is `ready` and the final comparison
matches the selected passages without copying their wording. Report
`Voice calibration: unresolved` when the evidence gate or comparison cannot be completed.
Name what is missing.

## Check paragraph continuity

Read each paragraph for its job in this assignment, then follow the sentence sequence.
Check whether an example interrupts an explanation, a qualification detaches from its
claim, or a punchline loses its setup. Shared subject matter alone does not prove flow.
Move the smallest necessary span to restore the relationship; keep unaffected wording
when the author requested a local correction. Do not impose one universal paragraph order.

Read adjacent prose paragraphs in pairs. Flag a run of three or more paragraphs when every
paragraph resets to an independent claim, subject, or citation and the prior thought does
not carry into the next one. The strongest tell is reorderability: if the paragraphs could
be shuffled like slides without changing the argument, the prose has card-deck flow.

Fix the argument movement. Carry a subject forward, develop or qualify the prior claim,
merge paragraphs that form one spoken thought, or restore connective tissue supported by
the calibration passages. Do not repair the run by sprinkling transition words over the
same independent cards.

Do not flag:

- a one-line punchline that depends on the paragraph before it
- a deliberate scene cut or section boundary
- steps, lists, code blocks, or other intentionally modular material
- an earned digression that the author uses in the selected mode
- a paragraph that changes direction while explicitly carrying or qualifying the prior idea

Related ideas may share one sentence. A paragraph may develop several facets of one thought.
The one-idea and straight-line defaults do not authorize mechanical splitting or removal of
earned connective tissue.

## Report independent states

The draft summary reports these states separately:

- `Author preservation`: `pass`, `needs repair`, or `unresolved`, with source-to-revision
  passage evidence for consequential changes and retained functions
- `Mechanical sweep`: `clean`, `findings`, `tool error`, or `not run`
- `Manual anti-pattern review`: `complete`, `unresolved`, or `not run`
- `Voice calibration`: `calibrated`, `unresolved`, or `not applicable`
- `Assignment mode`: the selected mode
- `Calibration evidence`: each selected source, passage location, and authorship
- `Paragraph continuity`: `pass`, `fixed`, `unresolved`, or `not applicable`

None of these states implies another. A zero-hit script result establishes only
`Mechanical sweep: clean`.
